Access is not authority

A fictional situation

A community member recorded a language lesson for a local teaching project. Later, a copy appeared in a public online archive. We are not told what permissions were given or what community protocols apply. This case is invented for discussion and does not represent any community's rules.

Same recording. Same facts. Only the purpose changes.

Propose a use

"Training" means an AI system learns patterns from many recordings and texts. What it has learned can be hard to undo.

Sources behind this activity

This is an interpretation of course readings, not a case reported in them.

  • Belarde-Lewis et al. (2024): examine ownership, trust, and relational accountability in stewarding Indigenous research data. Read the Data Science Journal article
  • Hess & Ostrom (2007): a commons is not the same as open access. Understanding Knowledge as a Commons, introduction.
  • Illingworth & Forsyth (2026): training data is not fully disclosed, and there is no simple way to undo what trained models have consumed. Chapter 3, pp. 38–44.

Two further sources (the Indigenous Protocol and AI position paper and Heritage Lab's data sovereignty policy) shaped my thinking but have not been fully verified, so they are not used as evidence here.
