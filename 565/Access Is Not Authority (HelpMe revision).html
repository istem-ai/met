Access is not authority

Here, "authority" means who has a say over how knowledge is used. It does not mean who is expert enough to understand it.

A fictional situation

A community member recorded a language lesson for a local teaching project. Later, a copy appeared in a public online archive. We are not told what permissions were given or what community protocols apply. This case is invented for discussion and does not represent any community's rules.

Sources behind this activity

This is my interpretation of course readings, not a case reported in them. The scenario is fictional.

  • Belarde-Lewis et al. (2024): examine ownership, trust, and relational accountability in stewarding Indigenous research data, and discuss the CARE principle of Authority to Control. Read the Data Science Journal article
  • Hess & Ostrom (2007): the course reading notes that open access and a commons are not the same thing. Understanding Knowledge as a Commons, introduction. (Not checked against the full chapter text.)
  • Illingworth & Forsyth (2026): training data is not fully disclosed, and there is no simple way to undo what models have already consumed. Chapter 3, pp. 38–44. This is stated in a copyright context and does not settle whether a specific recording could be removed.

Two further sources I considered (the Indigenous Protocol and AI position paper and Heritage Lab's data sovereignty policy) have not been fully verified, so they are not used as evidence here.
